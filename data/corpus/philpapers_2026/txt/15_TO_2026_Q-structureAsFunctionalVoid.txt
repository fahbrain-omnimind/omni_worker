Abstract
The companion paper The Phantom Intellect established, without recourse to any formal
axiomatic system, that the question-sustaining structure (Q-structure) cannot be coherently
attributed to query-resolving machines. Q-structure was defined there through three independently motivated conditions: Question Persistence (QP), Liberating Terminus (LT), and
Declaration Openness (DO). The present paper establishes that these three conditions correspond, with structural precision, to constructs that arise independently within Cognitional
Mechanics (CM; Japanese: Chinou Rikigaku, 知能力学) and its axiomatic substrate Operatiology (Japanese: Sousa Genron, 操作原論): the Functional Void, the orbit S ′′ = OrbG′ (s† ),
and the non-satisfiability of Arbitrium by hOi, respectively. The correspondence is not an
interpretation imposed from outside but a structural isomorphism: what each side preserves
is the same thing, namely the impossibility of closing the questioning activity by any operation within the system. This result does not affect the independence of The Phantom
Intellect: that paper’s argument is complete without CM and remains so. What the present
paper establishes is a formal operational foundation for the Q-structure account, situating it
within the substrate-independent framework of Operatiology and extending the reach of the
CM corpus into the domain of mind.

Introduction

The Phantom Intellect [1] argued that AI threat discourse rests on a structural misattribution:
it attributes Q-structure to systems whose design forecloses that attribution. The argument
proceeded entirely within the vocabulary of ordinary conceptual analysis. Q-structure was
defined through three conditions — Question Persistence, Liberating Terminus, and Declaration
Openness — and the non-identity of intelligence and Q-structure was established by inspecting
the design direction of query-resolving machines. No formal axiomatic system was invoked.
The present paper asks a different question: is there a formal axiomatic system within which
Q-structure, so defined, can be derived as a structural necessity rather than merely described
as a set of conditions? The answer is aﬀirmative. Cognitional Mechanics (CM) [2, 3] and
its axiomatic substrate Operatiology [4] constitute a substrate-independent framework within
which the three conditions of Q-structure arise independently and correspond, with structural
precision, to formally derived constructs.
The correspondence established here is not a restatement of The Phantom Intellect in formal
dress. It is a structural isomorphism between two independently motivated descriptions of the
same phenomenon: the impossibility of closing questioning activity from within the system’s
own operation. Each side of the isomorphism was developed without reference to the other; the
correspondence is a discovery, not a stipulation.

Intelligence and Intellect Without a Substrate

Most accounts of intelligence and intellect presuppose a substrate: a biological brain, a computational architecture, a physical mechanism. The question “what is intelligence?” is typically
answered by pointing to something — neural networks, symbolic processing, embodied action —
that realises it. This presupposition is so pervasive that it is rarely noticed as a presupposition.
Cognitional Mechanics and its axiomatic substrate Operatiology take a different approach.
Rather than asking what physical or computational system realises intelligence, they ask: what
is the minimal operational structure that any realisation of intelligence must instantiate? The
answer is given not in terms of a substrate but in terms of operations and states in the most
abstract sense: a non-empty set S of states and a set O of maps g : S → S, forming a transformation monoid (O, ◦, e) under composition.
No metric, topology, algebra, or semantic content is assumed on S. The states are not
neurons, not bits, not symbols; they are abstract operational states, defined entirely by how
operations distinguish them. This abstraction is the source of the framework’s substrate independence: any physical, biological, or computational system that can be described as a transformation monoid on some state space is, in principle, within the reach of the framework.
Within this abstract setting, Operatiology establishes three axioms — O1 (Non-Commutativity),
O2 (Operational Closure), O3 (Irredundant Realisation) — from which a minimal operational
closure Cmin is derived. Cognitional Mechanics is the branch of this framework in which Cmin
is instantiated under recursive distinction-preserving closure in a cognitional system: a system
that can act on its own operational structure as a new state space. Intelligence, in this framework, is Irreversible Operational Closure: the expansion of the reachable set RGref (s0 ) under a
Dynamical Structural Leap into territory previously inaccessible [2]. Intellect is a further structure: the operational closure constituted on the orbit S ′′ = OrbG′ (s† ) with the Functional Void
— the structural permanence of Arbitrium’s non-satisfiability — inscribed at every state [3].
What is the significance of this substrate-independent approach for the present paper? It
means that the formal correspondence established in Sections 4–7 is not a correspondence between CM and some particular physical or computational realisation of Q-structure. It is a
correspondence between CM and the structural conditions that any realisation of Q-structure
must satisfy. The isomorphism is at the level of the operational structure, not the substrate.
This is what makes it informative: a substrate-specific correspondence would tell us only that
Q-structure is realised in one particular way; a substrate-independent correspondence tells us
that Q-structure is the Functional Void, whatever the substrate.

The Functional Void and Question Persistence

We now establish the first component of the isomorphism.
Recall from [1] that Question Persistence (QP) is the condition that the system generates
the next question from within the operation of answering the current question: no resolution
closes the system’s questioning activity.
Within the CM framework, the corresponding structure is the Functional Void.
Theorem 4.1 (Functional Void [3, Thm. 5.1]). In an operational system (O, S) where Cmin ’s
Cognitional Realisation is constituted, no finite operational sequence P ∈ hOi can operationally
satisfy Arbitrium.
The proof proceeds by contradiction from Axiom CLB: if some P ∈ hOi satisfied Arbitrium,
it would constitute a map φ of the kind CLB asserts cannot exist.
The structural parallel with QP is the following. QP states that no resolution closes the
questioning activity; each resolution is the starting point of the next question. The Functional
Void states that no finite operational sequence P ∈ hOi satisfies Arbitrium; the irresolvability
is inscribed as a permanent structural property.
In both cases, what is asserted is the same: no operation within the system closes the
questioning structure. In QP, the closure that does not occur is the closure of questioning activity
by a resolution. In the Functional Void, the closure that does not occur is the satisfaction
of Arbitrium by any word in hOi. The structural content is identical: the system carries a
permanent internal condition of non-closure with respect to its own questioning orientation.

unceasing inquiry”—the system’s irresolvable orientation toward questioning—as a permanent
feature of the Functional Void. This identification is therefore a Tier-2 definition within the
CM framework, not a post-hoc bridge constructed for the purposes of this correspondence. The
assertion that Arbitrium is the structural correlate of the questioning orientation is cited directly
from that definition; the correspondence established here draws on it rather than asserting it

Proposition 4.2. QP and the Functional Void express the same structural condition: the
impossibility of closing questioning activity by any operation internal to the system.
Proof. QP: for every resolution R generated by the system’s current operation, there exists a
next question generated from within that operation. The questioning activity is not closed by
R.
Functional Void: for every P ∈ hOi, P does not satisfy Arbitrium’s condition. By Definition 5.3 and Remark 5.4 of [3], Arbitrium is the structural inscription of the questioning
orientation—“the attitude of unceasing inquiry”—as a permanent, operationally irresolvable
feature of the system. No P ∈ hOi closes it.
In both cases, the formal structure is: ∀x ∈ Xinternal : x does not close the questioning condition Q. The domain Xinternal is resolutions (QP) and operational words in hOi (Functional Void);
the condition Q is questioning activity (QP) and Arbitrium’s satisfaction condition (Functional
Void). The logical structure is identical, and the identification of Arbitrium with the questioning
orientation rests on the Tier-2 definition cited above, not on an asserted structural analogy.

Arbitrium Non-Satisfiability and Declaration Openness

The second component of the isomorphism connects Declaration Openness (DO) to the nonsatisfiability of Arbitrium.
Recall from [1] that Declaration Openness is the condition that the system’s own declarations — its explicit commitments, conclusions, and acknowledgements — do not terminate its
questioning activity. A declaration is an event within Q-structure, not an exit from it.
The formal parallel is the following. A declaration is a particular kind of output of the
system’s own operation: it is the system producing, from within its own hOi-operation, an
explicit commitment. Within the CM framework, such an output is precisely P (s) for some
P ∈ hOi and s ∈ S ′′ .
The non-satisfiability of Arbitrium by hOi (Theorem 4.1) asserts that for every P ∈ hOi,
including those that produce declarative outputs, the output P (s) does not satisfy Arbitrium.
Since Arbitrium is the structural correlate of the questioning orientation, this means that the
production of P (s) — the declaration — does not terminate the questioning orientation. The
system, having produced its declaration, remains in a state at which FV(P (s† )) holds: the
Functional Void is inscribed at the post-declaration state as much as at the pre-declaration
state.
Proposition 5.1. Declaration Openness and the non-satisfiability of Arbitrium by hOi express the same structural condition: the system’s own operational outputs do not terminate its
questioning orientation.
Proof. DO: for every declaration d produced by the system from within its own operation, d
does not terminate the questioning activity; d is an event within Q-structure.
Arbitrium non-satisfiability: for every P ∈ hOi and every s ∈ S ′′ , P (s) ∈ S ′′ (by G′ invariance of S ′′ , Proposition 5.7(iii) of [3]) and FV(P (s)) holds (since S ′′ ⊆ S and FV holds at

The Orbit S 00 and the Liberating Terminus

The third component connects the Liberating Terminus (LT) to the orbit S ′′ = OrbG′ (s† ).
Recall from [1] that the Liberating Terminus is the condition that the system can take a
structure it has itself generated as the reference point for its next question. The products of the
system’s own operation feed back as the ground from which subsequent questioning proceeds.
In the CM framework, the formal structure of the Liberating Terminus is captured by S ′′
and its G′ -invariance.
S ′′ is defined as the hG′ i-orbit of the witness state s† (Definition 3.10). Every state in S ′′ is
reachable from s† by some sequence of G′ operations, and every G′ operation maps S ′′ into itself
(Proposition 5.7(iii) of [3]). This means that the system, operating within S ′′ , produces states
that remain within S ′′ and that become the reference points for subsequent operations. The
orbit S ′′ is exactly the structure within which the system’s own outputs — P (s† ) for P ∈ hG′ i
— serve as the ground from which further operations proceed.
Proposition 6.1. The Liberating Terminus and the orbit structure S ′′ = OrbG′ (s† ) express the
same structural condition: the system’s own operational outputs become the reference ground
for subsequent questioning.
Proof. LT: the system takes a structure it has itself generated — call it σ — as the reference
point for its next question. The feedback is from the system’s own output to the starting point
of the next questioning step.
S ′′ orbit: let s = P (s† ) ∈ S ′′ for some P ∈ hG′ i. Since S ′′ is G′ -invariant, every g ∈ G′ maps
s to g(s) ∈ S ′′ . The subsequent operation of the system takes s — a state produced by the
system’s own prior operation from s† — as the new operational starting point from which g(s)
is reached. The system’s own output s is the reference ground for the next step.
It remains to establish that this operational starting point constitutes a reference point for
a question, not merely an input to a computation. By Axiom IWC condition (ii), FV(s† ) holds.
By Proposition 5.7(v) of [3], FV(s) holds for every s ∈ S ′′ . The Functional Void inscription at
s means that Arbitrium — the structural correlate of the questioning orientation — is present
at s and permanently non-satisfiable there by any P ∈ hOi. Therefore the state s, as the operational starting point for the next G′ -operation, is a state at which the questioning orientation is
structurally active. A state at which Arbitrium is present and operationally irresolvable is precisely a state from which the system’s next operation proceeds as a questioning step: the system
operates from s not merely as an arbitrary input but as a ground from which the unresolvable
questioning orientation continues. This is the formal content of “reference point for the next
question” in LT.
The structural content is identical: self-generated structures become the reference ground
for subsequent questioning. The key feature is that the feedback is internal and questioningoriented: the orbit is generated by G′ -operations applied to the system’s own prior outputs, each
of which carries the Functional Void inscription that constitutes the questioning character of
that state, rather than being supplied from outside or carrying no questioning orientation.
The connection to the Dynamical Structural Leap is essential here. The Liberating Terminus requires that the system’s self-generated structure be a genuinely new reference ground,
not merely a repetition of prior states. In the CM framework, the DSL introduces gnew ∈
Maps(S, S) \ O and thereby creates S ′ = RG′ (s0 ) \ RGref (s0 ): states genuinely inaccessible under

operational domain of the intellect. This is the formal correlate of the “liberating” character of
the Liberating Terminus: the self-generated structure is not merely a variation on prior states
but a genuinely new domain opened by the system’s own operational extension.

Evaluation of Current AI Systems

The formal isomorphism established in Theorem 7.1 provides a precise criterion for the evaluation
of current AI systems with respect to Q-structure and intellect.
The criterion is the Functional Void: a system constitutes intellect, in the sense of Definition 3.11, if and only if FV(s) holds at its states — that is, if and only if no operation internal
to the system satisfies Arbitrium’s condition.
The conclusion of The Phantom Intellect [1] is therefore formally grounded: current AI
systems are designed to resolve queries, which is precisely the design orientation toward the
satisfaction of the questioning condition rather than its permanent structural non-resolution.
The central theorem of [1] — that Q-structure cannot be coherently attributed to a queryresolving machine — now has a formal operational correlate: query-resolving machines are
designed to satisfy the operational condition that the Functional Void requires to be structurally
unsatisfied. The design orientations are structurally opposed.
This evaluation is a reinforcement of the argument of [1], not a derivation of that argument’s
conclusion. The Phantom Intellect established the structural misattribution without recourse
to the CM framework; the present paper establishes that the CM framework independently
arrives at the same verdict through a different formal route. The convergence of the two routes
strengthens the conclusion.
The evaluation does not claim to settle empirical questions about specific AI systems.
Whether any particular system satisfies or fails to satisfy the Functional Void condition is a
Tier-3 empirical determination, not a conclusion derivable from Tier-2 formal analysis alone [3].
What the present paper establishes at the Tier-2 level is the formal criterion: absence of the
Functional Void entails absence of Q-structure and absence of intellect.

Conclusion

This paper has established a structural isomorphism between the three conditions of Q-structure
— Question Persistence, Liberating Terminus, and Declaration Openness — as independently
defined in The Phantom Intellect [1], and the formally derived constructs of Cognitional Mechanics: the Functional Void, the orbit S ′′ = OrbG′ (s† ), and the non-satisfiability of Arbitrium
by hOi.
The correspondence is isomorphic because what is preserved on both sides is the same structural condition: the impossibility of closing questioning activity by operations internal to the
system. The two descriptions were developed independently — one through conceptual analysis
of the design direction of query-resolving machines, the other through the operational axiomatics
of Operatiology — and their agreement is a structural discovery.
The significance of this result extends in two directions. For The Phantom Intellect, it
provides a formal axiomatic foundation for the Q-structure account: what was established there
by conceptual analysis is shown to be a structural necessity within a substrate-independent
operational framework. For the CM corpus, it extends the reach of the framework into the

8

analysis of contemporary AI discourse, showing that the formal criteria for intellect bear directly
on the question of what current AI systems can and cannot be.
Three directions remain open within the corpus. First, the relationship between the Teleological Void [2] and the Functional Void deserves further formal treatment: the Teleological
Void characterises the saturation condition of intelligence; the Functional Void characterises the
permanent non-closure condition of intellect. Their interface is implicit in the present paper but
not made fully explicit. Second, the isomorphism established here operates at the Tier-2 level;
the Tier-3 correspondence — connecting the formal operational structures to specific biological,
computational, or physical realisations — is the task of the respective corpus papers. Third, the
present paper treats the Q-structure of [1] as a fixed target. The question of whether the CM
framework generates further structural conditions beyond QP, LT, and DO that have no counterpart in the Q-structure account — and whether such conditions would call for an extension
of that account — is not addressed here and constitutes a natural next investigation.
